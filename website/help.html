How Roadie works.

Playlists, the play queue, Jump to File and the visualization.

Playlists and the playlist editor

  • The playlist editor is what’s playing. It’s the play queue, as in Winamp, not a saved playlist. Double-click a row to play it; press Q to play a row next; press J (or ⌘J) to find a track in it.
  • Your saved playlists are in the Library. Open it with ⌘L and choose Playlists. Playlists come from Music Assistant, if you use it; with only a music folder there’s no Playlists section. Save a queue to a file instead (below).
  • Put a playlist in the editor. Double-click it in the Library, right-click › Play (or Play Next / Add to Queue), drag it onto the playlist editor, right-click the list › Load Playlist…, or use the editor’s LIST › Load (choose Library Playlist… if it asks). In the menu bar it is Playlist › Load Playlist… (⌥⌘L) or Playback › Load Playlist from Library….
  • Empty the editor. Right-click the list › Clear Playlist (or REM › Remove All, LIST › New List, Playlist › Clear Playlist).
  • Every editor command is in the menu bar. Every command of the editor’s ADD, REM, SEL, MISC and LIST buttons is also in the Playlist menu in the menu bar, so it works on any skin. The sorts have shortcuts (⌥⌘1, ⌥⌘2, ⌥⌘3), as do Load Playlist… (⌥⌘L) and Save Playlist… (⌥⌘S).
  • Save what’s in the editor. LIST › Save, or Playback › Save Queue as Playlist…. On a Music Assistant queue it becomes a Music Assistant playlist. On your Mac’s own queue it’s saved as an M3U or PLS file, and LIST › Load (choose Playlist File… if it asks) or File › Load Playlist… opens it again.
  • Add songs to a saved playlist. In the Library, right-click tracks or an album › Add to Playlist ▸, or drag them onto the playlist in the Playlists list.
  • Dragging into the playlist editor. Artists, albums, tracks and playlists from the Library, and files and folders from Finder. On your Mac’s own queue they go in where you drop them. Library items dropped on a Music Assistant queue are added at the end. Hold ⌥ (Option) as you drop on the list to replace what’s there and start playing it; the list is shaded and says REPLACE PLAYLIST while ⌥ is held. On a Music Assistant queue a ⌥-drop of Library items replaces that queue. Files from Finder always go into your Mac’s own queue, so they can’t be dropped while a speaker is playing.

Jump to File

It searches only what’s in the playlist editor. If the editor is empty, add music from the Library or File › Open first.

Visualization

  • Open it. Window › Visualization (⇧⌘K), the main window’s menu › Visualization, or the clutter bar’s V. Settings › Visualization (⌥⌘K) can open it whenever playback starts.
  • Full screen. ⌥Return, a double-click or the green button; Esc comes back.
  • The overlay. The song, artist, time, what’s next and the output show over the picture, and hide with the pointer after a few seconds without mouse movement (Settings › Visualization sets how long, or Never). Move the mouse to bring them back.
  • Keys. → or Space: next preset. ← or Delete: previous. H: next preset without blending. R: random or in order. `: lock the preset. L: the preset list (type to search, Return to switch). T: the song title. F2, F3, F4, F5: title, time, preset name and frame-rate readouts. F1 or ?: every key. ⌥Return: full screen. Esc: close help or the list, else leave full screen. Z X C V B: previous, play, pause, stop, next. ↑ ↓: volume. Every command is also in the right-click menu.
  • Settings. Settings › Visualization: how often presets change, the blend time, the order, your own presets folder, the overlay’s timing and opening the visualization when playback starts.
  • Music Assistant speakers. The visualization needs the music playing on this Mac. While a speaker plays, the picture stays idle and the overlay says so.
  • Flashing lights. Some visualizations flash or change quickly; Roadie says so the first time the window opens. With Reduce Motion on (System Settings › Accessibility › Display), presets stop changing on their own (Settings › Visualization › Change them anyway brings it back), changes crossfade, H blends instead of cutting, and the song title fades. With Dim Flashing Lights on, the picture is drawn darker and changes are slow crossfades. Roadie can’t detect flashes in a preset, so the dimming applies to every preset.
  • VoiceOver. While VoiceOver runs the overlay and pointer never hide, and changing the preset with a key or the list announces its name.
