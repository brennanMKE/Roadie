How Roadie works.

Playlists, the play queue and Jump to File.

Playlists and the playlist editor

  • The playlist editor is what’s playing. It’s the play queue, as in Winamp, not a saved playlist. Double-click a row to play it; press Q to play a row next; press J (or ⌘J) to find a track in it.
  • Your saved playlists are in the Library. Open it with ⌘L and choose Playlists. Playlists come from Music Assistant, if you use it; with only a music folder there’s no Playlists section. Save a queue to a file instead (below).
  • Put a playlist in the editor. Double-click it in the Library, right-click › Play (or Play Next / Add to Queue), drag it onto the playlist editor, or use the editor’s LIST › Load (choose Library Playlist… if it asks) or Playback › Load Playlist from Library… in the menu bar.
  • Save what’s in the editor. LIST › Save, or Playback › Save Queue as Playlist…. On a Music Assistant queue it becomes a Music Assistant playlist. On your Mac’s own queue it’s saved as an M3U or PLS file, and LIST › Load (choose Playlist File… if it asks) or File › Load Playlist… opens it again.
  • Add songs to a saved playlist. In the Library, right-click tracks or an album › Add to Playlist ▸, or drag them onto the playlist in the Playlists list.
  • Dragging into the playlist editor. Artists, albums, tracks and playlists from the Library, and files and folders from Finder. On your Mac’s own queue they go in where you drop them. Library items dropped on a Music Assistant queue are added at the end. Files from Finder always go into your Mac’s own queue, so they can’t be dropped while a speaker is playing.

Jump to File

It searches only what’s in the playlist editor. If the editor is empty, add music from the Library or File › Open first.
